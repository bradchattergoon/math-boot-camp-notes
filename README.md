## Who should use these notes?

These notes are designed as a mathematics boot camp specifically for incoming
PhD students to an Operations Research / Industrial Engineering PhD program.
The goal of these notes is to provide the mathematical foundations needed to be
successful in first-year Optimization and Stochastic Processes sequences.

There are two specific goals for Optimization:
  - A basic working knowledge of the theoretical foundation of gradient descent.
  - A basic working knowledge of optimization theory as it pertains to primal and dual problems, illustrated through linear programming.

There are two specific goals for Stochastic Processes:
  - A basic working knowledge of the Poisson process.
  - A basic working knowledge of Brownian motion.


In pursuit of both of these goals we only make an assumption that the reader
has a foundational quantitative background as might be obtained from an
Industrial Engineering, Applied Mathematics, or related, undergraduate
curriculum. However, we treat these goals through a rigorous mathematical lens,
building from mathematical fundamentals in Real Analysis, Linear Algebra, and
Probability theory with a proof-based approach.

These notes are not intended to be used primarily as reference material, but
rather primarily as learning material. With that in mind, the pedagogy employed
in the notes focuses on building intuition and motivation for the results
before developing the formal theorems and proofs. In particular, the notes are
structured for independent reading and learning by a reader, although they can
be paired with active teaching as a supplemental resource.

The pedagogical focus of these notes is on the parts of the foundations
specifically needed for the goals outlined above. This focus therefore is not
intended to give a thorough treatment of Real Analysis, Linear Algebra, or
Probability Theory as might be found in a course specifically dedicated to these
areas, and thus these notes are not a substitute for a course dedicated to any
of those areas.

The strength of these notes, however, is that they are tailored to what a
graduate student would need to know to have a strong understanding of the
specified topics as they pertain to the outlined goals, in as straightforward a
path as possible. With that in mind, these notes may also be of interest to
graduate students or advanced undergraduates in other areas of study which have
crossover with Optimization and Stochastic Processes who are looking for a
streamlined introduction to these ideas. These might be students in fields like
Statistics, Computer Science, Data Science, Economics, or Applied Mathematics.

As of this current version of these notes, the content covers the goals for
Optimization but does not yet provide any content for the goals related to
Stochastic Processes. The latter will be added in a future version.
